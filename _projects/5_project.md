There is a great deal we can learn about both pollinator behaviour and its interaction with agricultural land management by inspecting the carefully selected pollens collected by foraging bees. These pollen loads represent a physical record of a forager's past plant visitations, which can tell us how many types of flowers a forager has visited, of which species, and how similar her foraging patterns are to her sisters and conspecifics. We can learn whether flower use changes across time and space, and how this relates to lanscape composition. By comparing flower visitation and pollen use to availability, we can determine which plants are most preferred and might be a good fit for pollinator seed mixes.
